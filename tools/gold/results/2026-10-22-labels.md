## Labeller calibration against ground truth (the number that bounds the rest)

250 ground-truth items (stratified by source, rule and truth; Juliet
cross-file cases left out), labelled blind: comments blanked, Juliet and
OWASP case names replaced, paths neutralised.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **final (two labellers + adjudicator)** | 250 | 238 | 12 | 0.996 | **0.993 (143/144)** | **0.962** | 0.010 | 0.966 |
| prosecutor alone | 250 | 237 | 13 | 0.992 | 0.986 | 0.951 | 0.020 | 0.959 |
| defender alone | 250 | 247 | 3 | 0.980 | 0.967 | 0.926 | 0.049 | 1.000 |
| unanimous items only | 239 | 236 | 3 | 0.996 | 0.993 | 0.961 | 0.010 | 1.000 |

By source (final): OWASP Java 88/88 right; Juliet 106/106 decided right
(12 unsure); OWASP Python 37/38 (one false TP); CVE fixes 6/6. Cohen's κ
prosecutor vs defender 0.913; final label vs truth 0.991. Every `fp` call
was right (94/94).

The one false TP: OWASP Python `BenchmarkTest01096`, `pickle.loads` on a
value that, by a constant-condition trick (`bar = "This should never
happen"; if 'should' in bar: bar = param`), *does* carry the request path.
The benchmark marks it safe; the labellers traced the flow and called it
exploitable. Reading the code, the labellers look right and the benchmark's
label looks wrong. It still counts against them here.
*Correction (2026-10-23): the benchmark is right. The route is fixed, so
`request.path.split("/")[1]` is always `"benchmark"`. The benchmark marks
94 of the 112 cases that read it safe, and the other 18 are categories
that need no tainted input. See `2026-10-23-calibration.md`.*

**Plainly: the labellers' `tp` precision on ground truth is 0.993, but its
95% lower bound is 0.962, below 0.99.** On its own, a model-labelled sample
cannot support a 0.99 lower bound for any rule yet: the labels are
themselves only known to be right to about 96% at 95% confidence. Showing
labeller `tp` precision of at least 0.99 at 95% needs about 380 `tp` calls
on ground truth with no error. Calibration also covers only what the ground
truth covers (injection, weak randomness and hashing, deserialisation). For
secrets, tests, stubs, docs drift, supply chain, resilience and concurrency
there is no external truth, so the model labels there are uncalibrated.

## Headline precision

- **Gold sample** (24 exemplary repositories, 3,160 in-scope findings;
  metrics and census rows excluded), population-weighted: **0.349 (95% CI
  0.313-0.384)** on 589 decided labels. Held-out repositories alone: 46 TP /
  89 FP (0.341 unweighted). Prosecutor-defender κ 0.89; 32 of 600 went to
  the adjudicator; 11 unsure. This replaces the "1 in 3 is noise" budget
  assumption with a measurement, and it is worse: about 2 in 3 findings on
  exemplary code are wrong.
- **AI corpus**: 81 TP / 6 FP. 65 of the 81 are dependency pinning. The
  model labels disagree with the earlier hand-written `false_alarms` on 14
  of 19 shared items: 12 are `go.db-call-no-deadline` on embedded SQLite
  (literally true, but the corpus labeller ruled it out of scope), plus 1
  `drift.documented-env-var-absent` and 1 `typescript.promise-catch-noop`.
  The claim wording decides these; see open questions.
- **Ground truth** (OWASP, Juliet, CVE): pattern-only injection rules are
  near coin-flip on benchmarks built to be half safe: `java.sql-string-built`
  0.431, `java.command-injection` 0.432, `java.xss-unescaped-html` 0.801.
  Exact-signature rules are clean: `java.weak-random-for-secret` 218/218
  (lower bound 0.983), `python.insecure-random-for-secret` 99/99 (0.963),
  `python.weak-password-hash` 71/71 (0.949).

## Which rules or categories could plausibly reach a lower bound of 0.99

A Wilson lower bound of 0.99 with no FP needs about 380 TP labels (about 470
with one FP). Nothing reaches it today. Candidates, by evidence:

| Rule | Evidence | Now | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `java.weak-random-for-secret` | ground truth 218/218 | LB 0.983 | ~165 more TPs with 0 FP (more OWASP-like cases or CVE pairs), and real-code labels |
| `python.insecure-random-for-secret` | ground truth 99/99 | LB 0.963 | ~280 more |
| `python.weak-password-hash` | ground truth 71/71, model 1/1 | LB 0.949 | ~310 more |
| `assurance.blanket-suppression` | model 22/22 | LB 0.851 | volume; uncalibrated category |
| `supply.unpinned-python-dep`, `supply.unpinned-action` | model 22/22, 18/19 | 0.851, 0.754 | volume, plus a claim-scope decision |
| `resource.k8s-*` (7 rules) | model 4/4 each, one manifest family | ~0.51 | far more distinct manifests |
| `ruby.rescue-exception`, `ruby.skipped-spec` | model 6/6, 5/5 | 0.61, 0.57 | volume |

Categories: no category is near 0.99. `iac` (27/27, lower bound 0.875) is
the only one with no FP, from one repository's manifests.
`auth-crypto` is 0.927 overall, but almost all of that is the OWASP
weak-random and hash truth; on real code `authored.security-check-disabled`
is 3/29. Far below the bar on gold code: `secrets` 1/102 (nearly all test
fixtures and examples), `stubs` 2/33, `docs-drift` 9/49, `concurrency`
1/18, and `injection` on real code (`ruby.sql-injection` 0/7,
`go.sql-injection` 0/3, `python.sql-injection` 4/10).

**Verdict.** A rule-only gate cannot reach 99% on any category with what
exists. The exact-signature rules above are the `offline`-profile
candidates once they have the volume. Everything else needs the Phase 2
verifier, and that in turn needs labeller calibration with a lower bound of
0.99, which needs more ground truth.

## Open questions

1. **Claim scope decides several rules.** `go.db-call-no-deadline` on
   embedded SQLite, `supply.unpinned-npm-dep` with a lockfile, and tag-pinned
   first-party actions are all literally true and arguably harmless. The
   claims in `label_models.CLAIMS` encode one choice: lockfile and library
   ranges are FP, first-party actions and embedded databases are TP. The
   rule owners should confirm or change each claim before these labels
   enter the ledger. Changing a claim changes the excerpt hash, so the item
   is relabelled.
2. **More ground truth before trusting model labels at 0.99.** OWASP/Juliet
   have plenty of unlabelled `tp` cases left (2,826 Juliet TPs, 1,056 OWASP).
   About 400 more blind calibration items would test whether labeller `tp`
   precision clears 0.99 at 95%, for about $15. This only covers injection
   and crypto. Other categories need their own truth (for example secrets:
   GitHub secret-scanning advisories; tests: mutation results from
   `tools/mutate_tests.py`).
3. **A cross-vendor labeller.** Prosecutor and defender are the same model.
   Their κ of 0.91 bounds prompt-to-prompt variance, not a shared blind spot.
   The plan's open-weights second opinion is the check for that.
4. **Held-out AI corpus.** fixtures/ai-corpus is development data. A second
   corpus from a different generator model is still needed for a held-out
   AI-code figure.
