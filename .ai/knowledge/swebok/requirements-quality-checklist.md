# Requirements Quality Checklist

Use this checklist before implementing, approving, or marking a requirement
complete.

## Identity

- Requirement has a stable ID.
- Requirement has a clear title.
- Requirement has a category.
- Requirement has an owner or source when applicable.

## Clarity

- Requirement states what is needed.
- Requirement avoids unnecessary implementation detail.
- Requirement defines actors, users, systems, or triggering events when needed.
- Terms are understandable or defined.
- Ambiguous adjectives and adverbs are avoided or made measurable.

## Completeness

- Acceptance criteria are present.
- Validation method is present.
- Documentation impact is identified.
- Scope and exclusions are clear.
- Assumptions and dependencies are listed.

## Feasibility

- Requirement is technically feasible within known constraints.
- Risks are listed.
- Priority is assigned.
- Conflicts with existing requirements or rules are resolved or documented.

## Verifiability

- A human or automated check can determine whether the requirement is satisfied.
- Non-functional expectations are measurable or reviewable.
- Edge cases are listed when behavior depends on input, state, permissions, or
  environment.

## Traceability

- Requirement links to affected files, docs, tests, or design sections when
  known.
- Implementation changes can be traced back to the requirement ID.
- Changelog or release notes can reference the requirement when public-facing.
