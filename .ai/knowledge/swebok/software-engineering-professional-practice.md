# Software Engineering Professional Practice

This knowledge pack adapts common software engineering professional practice for
OmniContext. Use it when work involves ethics, communication, collaboration,
accountability, competence, or responsible use of AI-assisted engineering.

## Purpose

Professional practice is how engineers apply technical skill responsibly. It
includes communication, ethics, teamwork, accountability, lifelong learning,
stakeholder respect, and responsible decision-making.

In OmniContext, professional practice guidance should help an LLM or human
engineer:

- Communicate honestly.
- Avoid overstating certainty.
- Respect privacy, security, and intellectual property.
- Make risks visible.
- Preserve user work.
- Escalate decisions that require human authority.

## Professional Responsibilities

- Be honest about evidence, uncertainty, and validation.
- Protect confidential and sensitive information.
- Avoid unauthorized destructive actions.
- Respect existing project ownership and user changes.
- Identify conflicts between user requests, safety, law, policy, and quality.
- Keep professional judgment visible in final reports.

## AI-Assisted Practice

For AI-assisted engineering:

- Do not invent validation results.
- Do not hide skipped checks.
- Do not silently broaden scope.
- Do not treat model confidence as evidence.
- Do not expose secrets or private data.
- Use handoff packets when switching models or people.
- Keep local-only notes out of public artifacts when instructed.

## Communication

Good engineering communication is specific, concise, and evidence-backed. It
names what changed, why it changed, how it was validated, what remains risky,
and what should happen next.

## LLM Operating Rules

When professional judgment matters:

1. Read `.ai/knowledge/swebok/software-engineering-professional-practice.md`.
2. Use `.ai/knowledge/swebok/professional-practice-checklist.md`.
3. Escalate destructive, sensitive, legal, financial, or security-critical
   decisions to a human.
4. Report uncertainty and residual risk clearly.
